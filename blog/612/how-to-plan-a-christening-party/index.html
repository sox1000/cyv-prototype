Family celebrations

How to plan a christening party

ChooseYourVenue editorial team Published 14 October 2026 Updated 2 November 2026 6 min read

A christening party is usually short, relaxed and full of family: grandparents, godparents, friends with young children. The best ones keep things simple, with a room near the church, food that suits a lunchtime crowd and enough space for everyone to move around.

In brief

  • Book a venue a short drive from the church, ideally under 15 minutes.
  • Plan for two to three hours, starting soon after the service.
  • Choose a room a little bigger than your guest list, for prams and a buffet table.
  • Afternoon tea or a finger buffet suits most christenings.

Start with the church and the date

Everything follows from the service time. Once the church has confirmed it, you know when guests will arrive and how long the party can sensibly run. Most christenings take place on a Sunday, often after the morning service, so the party becomes a long lunch.

Keep the journey short. Guests travelling with babies, and older relatives, appreciate a venue they can reach in a few minutes, with parking or a drop-off close to the door.

Choose the right kind of venue

Most christening parties are held in one of four places:

  • A pub function room: informal, often close to the church, and usually the most affordable.
  • A hotel: easy for guests travelling from further away, with catering and staff on hand.
  • A village or church hall: plenty of space and freedom to bring your own food, but more to organise yourselves.
  • A restaurant with a private room: good for smaller, sit-down gatherings.

Size the room honestly

Count everyone, including babies and children, then choose a room with a little more capacity than your list. The extra space goes on a buffet or cake table, a corner for prams and car seats, and room for small children to move about safely.

A room that is a little full feels lively; one that is half empty feels flat. If you are unsure of numbers, ask the venue whether a partition or a smaller room is available.

Get the timing right

Two to three hours is plenty for most christening parties. Start within half an hour of the service ending, so guests go straight from church to the venue, and plan to finish by mid-afternoon, before young children tire.

Daytime hire often costs less than an evening booking, and some venues waive the room fee when you spend a set amount on food and drink. Ask about both when you enquire.

Food and drink

Afternoon tea and finger buffets are the most popular choices: easy to eat standing up, and simple to serve to guests who arrive at slightly different times. For a smaller group, a sit-down lunch works well in a restaurant or hotel.

  • Ask whether the venue has children's portions or a children's menu.
  • Check how the venue handles allergies and dietary needs.
  • Agree whether you can bring your own christening cake, and whether there is a cake-cutting charge.
  • A drink to toast the baby is traditional; a jug of soft drinks on each table helps families.

Make it easy for families

A few details make a big difference to parents and older guests:

  • Highchairs and baby-changing facilities
  • Step-free access and a lift if the room is upstairs
  • Parking close by, or a drop-off point at the door
  • A quiet corner or side room for feeding and naps
  • Outdoor space, if the weather is kind

Questions to ask before you book

  1. Is the room private, and for how long is it ours?
  2. Is there a room hire fee, a minimum spend, or both?
  3. What food packages do you offer for daytime parties?
  4. Can we bring our own cake and decorations?
  5. Do you have highchairs, baby-changing and step-free access?
  6. What deposit do you take, and what is the cancellation policy?

Christening party FAQs

How far ahead should we book a christening venue?

Enquire as soon as your church date is confirmed. Weekend lunchtimes are the first to go, so contact two or three venues at once and hold the one that suits you.

Do we need a private room?

For most christenings, yes. A private room gives you somewhere to set out food and cake, lets children move about freely and keeps speeches and photos simple.
